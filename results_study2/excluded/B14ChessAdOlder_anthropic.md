Good morning, everyone, and thank you for coming along. I'd like to take a few minutes to tell you about a game that has been played for around fifteen hundred years, and which I believe is one of the finest pastimes anyone can take up at our stage of life. I'm talking about chess.

Now, I can already hear some of you saying, "Chess is for clever young people, and I'd never keep up." Let me put that worry to rest. You don't need to be a genius, and you don't need a lifetime of practice. You only need curiosity and a willingness to have a go. Plenty of people learn the moves in an afternoon and spend the rest of their lives enjoying the game.

So what's in it for us? First, chess is a wonderful workout for the mind. Just as a daily walk keeps the legs in good order, a game of chess keeps the thinking part of us nimble. You have to plan ahead, remember what has happened, weigh up choices, and picture what might come next. Researchers have long been interested in whether mentally active hobbies help protect memory and sharpness as we age, and many doctors encourage activities of exactly this sort. While no game can promise miracles, it certainly does no harm to keep the brain busy and engaged.

Second, chess is a grand way to meet people. Many community centres, libraries and clubs run chess afternoons, and the atmosphere is usually friendly and unhurried. You sit down across from a stranger, and within a few moves you're chatting like old friends. For anyone who finds the days a little quiet, or who has lost a partner or moved to a new area, that companionship can mean a great deal.

Third, chess teaches patience and calm. There's no rush, no shouting and no noise. You take your time, think it through, and enjoy the quiet. Many people find it soothing, a pleasant break from the worries of the day. And when you lose a game, as we all do, you learn to shrug it off, shake hands and try again, which is a fine habit for life in general.

Fourth, it's wonderfully affordable. A board and a set of pieces cost very little, and you can play at the kitchen table, in the garden or on a park bench. If you prefer, you can play on a tablet or computer against a patient opponent who never minds how long you take, or even with a friend across the world.

Finally, chess is a game that never runs out. However long you play, there's always something new to discover, always another clever trick to learn. That sense of progress and achievement is a joy at any age.

So please, come and give it a try. We'll set up the pieces, show you how each one moves, and sit beside you for your first game. There's no pressure and no exams. Just good company, a cup of tea, and a chance to give your mind a pleasant stretch. I do hope you'll join us.
